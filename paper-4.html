Abstract

Choosing meaningful method names is essential for code readability and maintainability. When developers write or refactor code, select- ing appropriate names that clearly convey the method’s purpose can be challenging and time-consuming. Recently, improvements in Artificial Intelligence (AI), especially Large Language Models (LLMs), have created new opportunities to assist developers with method naming. Building on these improvements, our experiments tested LLMs’ ability to suggest good method names on the JHot- Draw project using seven LLMs: Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-3.5, GPT-4o, GPT-5, DeepSeek-R1, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and Claude Opus 4.1. We tested two ways: (1) comparing single method names, and (2) ask- ing each model to suggest 10 names for each method. The results show that different models perform differently at naming tasks. GPT-4o got the highest similarity scores with original methods in 9 out of 20 cases for single names. GPT-5 also did well, getting the highest similarity scores in 6 cases when comparing all 10 names. DeepSeek-R1 got the highest similarity scores in 2 cases. Claude Opus 4.1 was especially good at naming UI and layout methods, with the highest scores in 6 cases. Gemini 2.5 Pro had mixed re- sults with highest scores in 2 cases. Claude 3.7 Sonnet created good names but never got the highest score. GPT-3.5 had the most prob- lems, often creating placeholder names with "temp" in them. All models followed Java’s camelCase naming style. No models used PascalCase, snake_case, or kebab-case. GPT-4o, GPT-5, and Claude Opus 4.1 were the best at creating names that make sense and follow naming rules. Overall, advanced LLMs gives developers a choice to use them for suggesting names.
